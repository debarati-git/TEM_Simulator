REFERENCE

TEM Image Capture

What a TEM image is

A 2D projection of how each region of your specimen scatters or absorbs electrons. Bright = electrons made it through; dark = electrons were scattered away by mass, thickness, or crystal orientation.

Where contrast comes from

The objective aperture picks which scattered beams reach the detector:

  • Mass-thickness — heavier or thicker regions darker.
  • Diffraction — crystallites at the Bragg condition appear dark in bright-field.
  • Phase — atomic-column interference (HRTEM). Needs careful defocus.
Focus & astigmatism

Focus sets sharpness. Astigmatism (stigmator) corrects for the lens being stronger in one direction. The wobbler oscillates focus to find eucentric height.

The DEF/STIG knobs

The same physical X/Y knobs on the left panel serve three functions depending on which mode is active: Beam Shift moves the beam position, Cond. Stig corrects condenser astigmatism, and Obj. Stig corrects objective astigmatism. Press the mode button first, then adjust.

Capturing the image

Insert the camera, start live view, raise the fluorescent screen so the beam hits the CCD, then acquire. Use the Live FFT to assess focus and stigmation — round rings = good; streaked = astigmatism remains.
